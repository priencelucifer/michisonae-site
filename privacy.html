MichiSonae privacy notice

Who we are

MichiSonae is made by Ricky Kashyap, an individual developer in India ("we"). Privacy questions and requests: rickykashyapbng4@gmail.com.

Summary

  • No account, name, phone number or email is needed.
  • MichiSonae does not record your route. It keeps a short summary of each ride (time, distance, estimated fuel, idling, cost) on your phone for 30 days, which you can turn off. Only if you turn on "Places on the Timeline" does it also keep where each ride started and ended, on your phone.
  • Road monitoring runs on your phone. Only a short report about a road hazard, detected by the phone or reported by you, is sent to the MichiSonae server.
  • The optional OBD-II adapter is read-only. Vehicle readings and fault codes stay on your phone.
  • You can delete everything the app stores on your phone at any time.

Ordinary location samples, raw motion-sensor data, raw OBD-II streams and microphone audio are not stored as history and are never uploaded. The microphone is used only while you report a hazard by voice in drive mode: Android's on-device speech recognizer turns your words into text on the phone, and the audio is not recorded, kept or sent.

What is sent to the MichiSonae server

Only over HTTPS:

  • Road hazard reports: a random report ID, an anonymous installation ID, the time, the location of the detected hazard (latitude, longitude and its accuracy), the vehicle speed and direction of travel at that moment, the hazard type, a severity and confidence score, the source (phone) and the detector version.
  • Hazards you report yourself: when you tap "Report a hazard" while stopped, or say one in drive mode, the app sends the same kind of report with the hazard type you chose, the location and speed at that moment. Spoken words are turned into text on the phone; only the resulting report is sent.
  • "Not felt" answers: when monitoring drives you right over a hazard that only one other driver has reported, going their way, and your phone feels no jolt, the app sends the same kind of answer as "Not there" (hazard ID, area code, answer, time, anonymous installation ID) without asking. Your location is not sent.
  • "Still there / Not there" answers: when you tap one while stopped near a hazard, the app sends the hazard's public ID, its area code, your answer, the time and the anonymous installation ID. Your location is checked on the phone to confirm you are near and stopped, but it is not sent.
  • Anonymous credentials: the app registers an anonymous installation and receives rotating access and refresh tokens. The server stores only hashes of the tokens.
  • Hazard map requests: the app downloads public hazard data for a coarse area (roughly 5 km x 5 km) around you. The area code is sent; your exact position is not.
  • Error reports (only when Settings > "Send error reports" is on; off by default in released versions): app crashes and errors, such as the OBD adapter disconnecting or the map failing to load. A crash report contains the type of error and where in the app's code it happened, never the error's own text. Reports contain no location, no OBD readings or raw adapter data and no account; they include the app version, Android version and phone model. They wait on the phone (at most 200) until uploaded and are written to the server's operations log, which keeps about the last 100 MB; they are not stored in a database.
  • Offline map download (only when you choose a state and tap Download or Update in Settings): the app downloads that state's map files, the same for every user. The server, the tunnel in front of it and the Cloudflare R2 storage the files come from see your IP address and which state's files this phone wants. Listing the states asks the server which ones it offers; which states are near you is worked out on the phone. While Settings is open on Wi-Fi with a state downloaded, the app checks at most once a week whether newer builds exist. No location, identifier or token is sent with any of these.
  • Server check: while the app is open it asks the server "are you up?" every 30 seconds, to tell you when it is not reachable. Nothing else is sent.
  • Network address: the server processes your IP address to block abuse. It is stored only as a keyed one-way hash in rate-limit counters and security audit records.

What the map provider receives

The Hazard map screen shows an OpenStreetMap map from OpenFreeMap (openfreemap.org). The map runs only while that screen is open, never in the background.

  • Map requests: while the map is open, the app downloads map data for the area you are looking at. OpenFreeMap receives your IP address and that area. It sets no cookies and the app sends no identifier with these requests.
  • The map software (MapLibre) sends no usage or location data of its own.
  • On the phone, the map keeps a cache of map data for areas you viewed. "Delete all local data" removes it.
  • With an offline map downloaded, the map inside that state is drawn from the files on your phone, and OpenFreeMap receives nothing for it. Road routes to fuel pumps are worked out on the phone; no routing service is contacted.

How reports are used

Reports from different anonymous installations are combined into public hazard markers. A marker is published once at least two different installations report the same area (about 150 m), or one installation reports it on trips at least 12 hours apart. It shows the hazard type, the average location of the reports, severity, confidence, number of contributors and when it was last reported, never an installation ID; a one-driver marker also shows the direction that driver was travelling. A marker with no new report or "still there" answer for 30 days is no longer published. A marker is also withdrawn once two different installations answer "not there" after its last report; a one-driver marker after one "not there" or "not felt" from someone else.

Retention on the server

  • Individual reports are deleted after 90 days (configurable from 30 to 90), except that the most recent report from each installation for a published hazard is kept while that hazard exists, because it supports the marker.
  • "Still there / Not there" answers: only your newest answer per hazard is kept, and it is deleted 30 days after you gave it.
  • Credentials expire (access 1 hour, refresh 30 days, credential family 90 days) and are revoked when you delete all data in the app.
  • Security audit records and rate-limit counters hold only keyed one-way hashes of network addresses and anonymous installation IDs, and are used only to block abuse. They are not yet deleted on a schedule.
  • Where the data is kept: the MichiSonae server runs on a computer we operate in India. Connections reach it through Cloudflare (a Cloudflare Tunnel), which carries the encrypted traffic, and offline map files are stored in Cloudflare R2. The database is backed up daily to two other computers we operate in India; each keeps the 14 newest backups, so a deleted record can remain in a backup for about two weeks.

Sharing

MichiSonae does not sell data and does not share it with advertisers or data brokers. Opening a fuel station, service centre or destination hands the search to your map app (for example Google Maps), which applies its own privacy policy. The Hazard map and Timeline screens use OpenFreeMap, as described above. Tapping a fuel pump or car service on the Hazard map hands its name and position to your map app. If you turn on "Place names", the Timeline asks your phone's geocoder (on most phones Google's, through Android) for the name of each place shown, which sends that place's position to the geocoder's provider while the screen is open. The names are not saved. "Share summary with a mechanic" opens Android's share sheet only when you tap it. The text contains your vehicle size and fuel type, and each fault code with its title, safe action and first and last seen dates. It does not include the vehicle nickname, VIN or any location. Where it goes is your choice. The providers involved are Cloudflare, which carries connections to the server and stores the offline map files, and OpenFreeMap, which serves map tiles.

Your choices

  • Delete all data: Settings, then "Delete all local data". This removes everything listed above from the phone and asks the server to revoke the anonymous credentials. Reports already accepted by the server are not linked to you by name and are removed by the retention schedule above.
  • Uploads only on Wi-Fi or when the battery is not low: Settings.
  • Location, Bluetooth and notification permissions can be withdrawn in Android settings at any time. Road monitoring needs location; OBD-II and the car's Bluetooth need Bluetooth only when you use them.

Children

MichiSonae is meant for drivers and is not directed at children. It is intended for people aged 18 and over.
